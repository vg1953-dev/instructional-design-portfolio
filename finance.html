Model architecture

From assumptions to management recommendations

Unit economics

Separates selling price, material, direct labor, variable overhead, and variable selling cost to calculate contribution margin and break-even volume.

Contribution / Unit = Selling Price − Variable Cost / UnitBreak-even Units = Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution / Unit

Budget vs. actual

Compares monthly planned and illustrative actual volume, realized price, material cost, contribution, and operating profit to isolate performance gaps.

Revenue Variance = Actual Revenue − Budget RevenueProfit Variance = Actual Op Profit − Budget Op Profit

Scenario planning

Models downside, base, and upside cases across volume, price, material, labor, variable overhead, and fixed costs.

Sensitivity analysis

Quantifies the incremental annual operating-profit effect of individual decision levers while holding other base assumptions constant.

Key findings

What the model says

Operating leverage is meaningful

The model ranges from a $4.9M operating loss in the downside case to a $9.14M operating profit in the upside case.

Volume is the largest sensitivity shown

An additional 10,000 units adds approximately $1.27M of annual operating profit at base unit economics.

Material cost deserves management attention

A $5 per-unit material-cost reduction improves annual operating profit by approximately $600K at base volume.

Break-even risk is manageable, not trivial

The base case breaks even at approximately 99K units against planned volume of 120K, producing a 17.3% margin of safety.
